Horses of myth 

Despite peoples insistence that dogs are mans best friend, horses have been just as instrumnetal to humanities progress and success. So it's no wonder that there's no shortage of folklore and myth surrounding them. You might know about unicorns and pegasi but what about Kirin? Or the Abada? Below is a list and simple summary of a handful of mythical horse-like creatures.

# Location Comments
1. Unicorn While commonly depicted as a horse today unicorns were originally thought to resemble donkeys and deer. Hailing from Europe they were often used as an allegory for Christ.
2. Pegasus A winged horse from Greek mythology that sprung from the severed neck of Medusa. Seen as a noble steed for the gods.
3. Kelpie Found in folklore from Scotland, the Kelpie is a semi-aquatic freshwater horse that lures victims to their demise via drowning so they can have a meal.  
4. Hippocampus A fully aquatic horse from Greek mythology that pulled the sea chariot of Poseidon.
5. Kirin A gentle beast found in ancient text from Japan, these horses are often given both horse and dragon-like features. They tread lightly so as to not harm a single blade of grass.
6. Longma  A dragon-like horse from Chinese mythology that is said to drive the carriages of gods and Emperors.
7. Abada A shy creature from African mythology with two crooked horns. They are said to have magical properties in said horns that could heal any ailment.
8. Shadhavarr  Found in Muslim writing from the SWANA region, an Abada has a long curved horn that plays a melody when wind blows through it that entrances anyone who hears it.
9. Slipnier An eight-legged horse from Norse mythology that is the child of Loki and a wild steed.
10. Indrik  A Russian variant of the unicorn described as being gentle and docile despite it’s size. When it runs it rattles the earth, causing earthquakes.
11. Hrimfaxi These horses of Norse mythology are said to sweat drops of morning dew as they pull the chariot of the night.
12. Skinfaxi These horses of Norse mythology are said to light up the sky with their manes as they pull the chariot of the day.
13. Cefflyl Dwr This mythical horse from Welsh folklore can evaporate into mist. Sometimes they tempt people to ride them only to drop them from the sky as they fly.  
14. Karkadann The “Lord of the Desert” in Persian myth this creature is said to be a cross between a horse and a rhinoceros whose horn is said to have medicinal properties.
15. Puca  An impish shapeshifter often taking the form of horse from Irish folklore. They aren’t evil and aren’t known for causing harm but can still give people a good scare.
16. Hippogriff Invented by an Italian poet the Hipogriff has the back half of a horse and the front half of an eagle. They are often seen serving noble men like knights and sorcerers.
